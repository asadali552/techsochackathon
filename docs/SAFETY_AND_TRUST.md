# Safety and trust

- **Reporter privacy.** Restrict raw descriptions, exact submitted coordinates, identities, credibility metadata, and evidence to authorized workflows. Generalize public coordinates and redact identifying details. Avoid recording precise personal trip locations in routine logs.
- **Verification.** New reports are private and pending. Review for personal data, abuse, and duplicate candidates before publishing a redacted marker. Label published unverified reports. Score corroborated and verified evidence only. Use corroborated for independent supporting evidence and verified for documented moderator confirmation.
- **False reports.** The API limits reports to ten per registered account per rolling hour. Reviewers exclude confirmed duplicates and rejected reports from public queries and scores. Record moderation rationale privately and provide a correction channel before public rollout.
- **AI limits.** Treat extracted fields and severity as provisional. Validate model output. Require supporting records and show provenance and uncertainty in explanations.
- **Coverage.** Show time range, freshness, and missing sources. Grey represents insufficient information. Green means relatively lower observed risk given adequate evidence. Neither an empty result nor a sparse reporting area establishes safety.
- **Official advisories.** Publish only after authority and source verification. Show issuance, effective period, and source link. Keep restrictions independent of travel time and traffic. Distinguish unavailable sources from a checked source with no matching restriction.
- **Retention.** Plan an initial 30-day purge for rejected report bodies and unused private evidence, subject to the team’s approved retention policy. Set review and expiry windows for public data by category. Stop presenting revoked or expired advisories as active while retaining provenance for authorized review.
- **Demo mode.** Label fictional incidents at the map, report, and assistant levels. Do not mix them with live evidence or imitate official advisories.

Phase 1 validates registered-user bearer tokens against Supabase Auth. SQL policies and column grants protect raw fields and restrict writes. New reports start pending and private. A trusted reviewer must redact the public summary before publishing it. Public list, detail, and nearby searches use generalized coordinates. Status changes and official-zone writes are administrative database operations. Mark reports verified only when an authorized reviewer records the confirming evidence and rationale in private credibility metadata.

Phase 3 computes cell scores from corroborated or verified public reports and uses the same rounded points as the public map. A published pending marker remains outside scoring. Two eligible reports meet the default prototype sample minimum, while smaller samples are grey. This heuristic cannot establish reporting completeness, crime probability, or future conditions. Live and fictional reports stay in separate queries.

Phase 4 sends only description text to a configured provider, with common contact identifiers, long numbers, and URLs redacted. Free-text redaction can miss names and other personal details. Reporters should omit identifying information. Keep provider access and retention aligned with the deployment's privacy requirements. AI output, inferred severity, and suspected duplicates stay private. Owners see their own suggestion, while configured moderators see duplicate references and evidence notes. Confidence estimates category fit rather than allegation truth. If a human confirms duplicate reports, keep one representative eligible and record the disposition of the others through the existing moderation process.

Phase 5 separates the weighted risk gradient from eligible report density. Missing risk evidence leaves the map clear. A route score reflects a recent sample of public reports near a driving corridor. Trip estimates, community evidence, road hazards, and stored advisories are shown independently. They cannot guarantee safe passage or establish the completeness of local coverage.

Journey endpoints go to the configured routing service, including in demo mode. The public FOSSGIS service logs query coordinates. MehfoozAI suppresses precise request coordinates in routine routing and nearby-search logs and keeps opaque route references for 15 minutes, with expired records purged on subsequent route lookups. [FOSSGIS routing service and privacy policy](https://routing.openstreetmap.de/about.html)

Phase 6 sends signed-in questions to the configured language model for intent interpretation. An explicit server setting allows unsigned demo questions to go to Gemini. It skips model calls for explicit coordinate pairs and questions matched by the contact redaction rules. Geocoding may send unresolved place names to the configured provider. Route generation still shares endpoints with OSRM. The chat tells users to use public places and shares map and route context only through visible controls. Conversation references expire after 15 minutes of inactivity or can be cleared. Nearby chat reports exclude pending and rejected records. Weather, emergency facilities, and live closure feeds are unavailable. Stored official restrictions remain available where records match.
